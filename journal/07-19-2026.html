July 19, 2026 Certs Learning

Google IT Support, Done — Network+, Still Waiting

Finished the Google IT Support certificate this week. Five courses, the last of them IT Security: Defense Against the Digital Dark Arts, which is a genuinely great title for a course that's really just threats, cryptography, and network security fundamentals dressed up with better marketing than most vendor material bothers with. Worth writing down, mostly for myself, because it's easy to let a finish line like this pass by unremarked when the next thing is already queued up.

What actually got done

It's not just the Google cert. Looking at the credentials that landed this month: Apache Web Server Administration, a TanStack Query course that mapped directly onto work already done in the capstone's service layer, and now the full Google IT Support sequence. All of that stacked on top of a job that's been running 45+ hours a week most weeks, the capstone build itself, and the research those sessions kept pulling me into — DNS resolution order, Handlebars templating, Zabbix's interface model, whatever the lab happened to break that particular day. None of that research was planned as coursework. It just kept turning into it.

The pattern that's actually worth noticing: the homelab and the formal coursework stopped being two separate tracks a while ago. A Coursera module explains the concept, the lab forces me to actually configure it and watch it fail, and the failure teaches more than the module did. Neither one is optional anymore for the other to make sense.

The one I'm avoiding

Here's the honest part. Network+ is the credential all of this is technically building toward, and the exam itself is still unscheduled. Not "haven't gotten to it" — I know the material, or close enough to it that another few weeks of drills would close the gap. It's just sitting there. I can point to the 45-hour work weeks, the capstone, the other courses, and all of that is true, but none of it is really the reason. Scheduling an exam means committing to a date I might fail against, and it's easier to keep collecting certificates that don't have that particular kind of deadline attached.

Worth naming plainly instead of quietly working around: the next entry in this direction should be a scheduled exam date, not another course completion. The courses are real progress. They're also, a little bit, the comfortable version of progress.

Where things stand

Capstone's live and stable, work is still work, and the learning hasn't slowed down even when the hours are stretched thin. That part I'm genuinely proud of. The Network+ exam gets its date next — no more courses standing in for it.
