March 26, 2026 Certs Learning

25 Certifications and a Full-Time Job

I work 45 hours a week managing a convenience store. I have a family. I'm finishing an associate's degree in front-end development. I run a four-node homelab in my house. And somewhere in there, between the last shift ending and the next one starting, I've earned 25 certifications since September 2025.

I'm not writing this to impress anyone. I'm writing it because looking back at that list recently, I didn't fully realize how much ground I'd covered until I saw it all in one place. This is an attempt to make sense of it — what I learned, why it matters, and what the pattern says about where I'm headed.

How it actually fits in a day

The honest answer is that something gives. It isn't family time — that's non-negotiable. It isn't work performance — I still have a job to do and people counting on me. What gives is sleep, sometimes, and most of the mindless scrolling that used to fill the gaps between things. Lecture videos play while I'm doing dishes. A course module runs during a lunch break. A lab session happens after everyone else has gone to bed.

The time was always there. I just stopped wasting as much of it.

I also stopped waiting until I had a long uninterrupted block to learn something. Thirty focused minutes is worth more than two hours of half-attention. That shift in how I think about study time is probably the most useful thing I've learned that doesn't show up on any credential.

Infrastructure and IT foundations

This is where the transition I'm working toward lives. Google's Technical Support Fundamentals and Bits and Bytes of Networking completed in March 2026 as part of the larger Google IT Support certificate. LinkedIn's Building a Home IT Lab and Computer Science Principles: The Internet filled in gaps earlier in the process. These aren't the most impressive credentials on the list but they're the most directionally important — they formalize knowledge I've been building hands-on in my homelab for months.

The homelab itself is the real credential in this category. Four Proxmox nodes, OPNsense routing, a VPN mesh, reverse proxying with SSL, uptime monitoring, a homelab dashboard with live infrastructure stats. I built all of it, broke most of it at least once, and fixed it. That experience doesn't fit on a LinkedIn profile but it informs everything else.

Development

React, GitHub, Web APIs, and database foundations round out the development side. The GitHub credentials in particular reflect something I use daily now — version control for code, for configuration files, for infrastructure documentation. It stopped being a tool I was learning and became a habit.

The Web APIs cert from NASBA and the database foundations course were deliberate choices to shore up the backend-adjacent knowledge that pure front-end coursework doesn't always cover. Understanding how an API works and how data is structured makes me a more useful front-end developer, not just someone who can style a page.

Design and UI

Seven Figma credentials sounds like a lot until you realize how deep the tool actually goes. Auto layout, variables, conditionals, prototyping, microinteractions, developer handoff — each one is a distinct workflow that takes time to internalize. Combined with the W3C's WCAG 2.2 accessibility certification, this represents a genuine investment in design craft, not just tooling familiarity.

Accessibility in particular is something I care about beyond the credential. Every project I build gets aria labels, keyboard navigation, reduced motion support, and skip links. That's not compliance checkbox behavior — it's just how interfaces should be built.

Agile and project management

This cluster came together in February 2026 and surprised me with how much of it I recognized from retail management. Scrum sprints look a lot like weekly operational planning. Jira boards look a lot like shift task lists. Agile's emphasis on iteration and feedback loops looks a lot like adjusting a store's workflow based on what the previous week showed you.

Twenty years of operations work turns out to be decent preparation for agile project management. The vocabulary is different but the instincts transfer. The Atlassian Professional Certificate and the three-part Jira Cloud series from PMI formalized that intuition into something I can articulate in a technical context.

What the list actually says

Scattered on the surface, intentional underneath. The Figma work feeds the front-end degree. The agile certs contextualize the management experience. The infrastructure credentials frame the homelab work. The GitHub and API knowledge connects the code side to the systems side. None of it happened in isolation.

The through-line is a person trying to build a complete picture of how software and systems work together — not just how to write a component, and not just how to keep a server running, but how those two things relate to each other and to the people using them. That's the role I want to grow into. Everything on the list is a step toward being able to do it.

What's next

CompTIA Network+ is the immediate target — the Google networking module is done, Professor Messer's materials are bookmarked, and the exam voucher is reimbursable through work. After that, the remaining Google IT Support modules, then Ansible, then whatever the homelab needs next.

The pace won't always be this aggressive. But right now, in this window before the internship and before the degree finishes, the momentum feels right. I'd rather be tired and prepared than rested and behind.
