April 5, 2026 Certs Learning

Studying With Something at Stake

I have roughly two weeks before a conversation with a network engineering manager at my employer. The email came last week — introduced, professional, open-ended. The timing works out to mid-April. That window feels both generous and short depending on the hour.

The most useful thing I can do with that time is study. Specifically Network+ — the CompTIA certification that formalizes the networking knowledge I've been building hands-on for the past several months. A network engineering manager will notice if you can speak intelligently about subnetting, VLANs, routing protocols, and firewall concepts. I can do that. The cert makes it verifiable.

How I'm studying

Professor Messer's free Network+ video series is the primary resource. His explanations are clear, his coverage of the N10-009 objectives is thorough, and the pace works well for someone who already has practical context for most of the material. I run through videos during lower-attention tasks and reinforce with practice questions afterward.

Practice exams are the real diagnostic tool. Taking timed exams and reviewing every wrong answer carefully is more valuable than passive re-reading. The domains where I'm weakest become obvious quickly and that's where the remaining study time goes.

Where the homelab connects

A significant portion of the Network+ objectives map directly to things I've already implemented. Subnetting — I manage a segmented local network with static assignments across a dozen services. Firewall rules — I've written and debugged rules for VPN traffic, websocket connections, and inbound and outbound filtering. DNS — I run a local resolver with custom host overrides for every internal domain. VPNs — I have a mesh network running direct and relay connections across multiple device types.

The gap is in the formal vocabulary and in areas the homelab doesn't naturally exercise — routing protocols, wireless standards, and security concepts that require enterprise scale to encounter organically. Structured study fills those in.

Practical experience without vocabulary is hard to communicate. Vocabulary without practical experience is hard to trust. The combination is the sweet spot.

The homelab cleanup

While keeping the study focus I also did some infrastructure housekeeping. I'd been running two Kanban tools in parallel to evaluate which one fit my workflow better. The decision was clear after a few days — one had a clean native integration with my dashboard and required no special proxy configuration. The other fought the setup every step of the way. Removed the losing one, released the resources back to the pool, updated monitoring and DNS accordingly.

That kind of cleanup is worth doing deliberately rather than letting unused services accumulate. Every running container is something to maintain, monitor, and back up. If it's not being used it's just overhead.

What the next ten days look like

Work obligations mid-month mean study happens in the margins — lunch breaks, after the family is settled, the half hour before a shift starts. The professional conversation happens in the second half of the month. Network+ exam scheduling comes after that depending on how things develop.

The goal isn't to be perfect before that conversation. The goal is to be clearly someone who is actively working toward this and has the evidence to prove it. That's achievable. That's the plan.
