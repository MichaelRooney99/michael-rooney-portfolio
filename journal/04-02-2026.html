April 2, 2026 Learning

A Door Opens

I received an email today from the Network Engineering Manager at my organization. He introduced himself, mentioned that his director had passed along my information, and offered to connect and share more about what the networking team does.

I've been working toward something like this conversation for months. It still felt a little surreal to see it in my inbox.

How it got here

I currently work as an assistant store leader. Over the past several months I've been quietly building toward a career transition into IT — finishing a front-end development degree, running a homelab, working through certifications, and staying connected with people inside the organization who knew where I wanted to go.

I didn't apply for a role or push for an introduction. This conversation came from people noticing the work I've been putting in and passing my name along. That part means just as much as the opportunity itself.

What I wrote back

I kept the reply concise. I introduced my background — the degree, the homelab, the specific technologies I've been working with — and mentioned that networking has become the area I'm most drawn to. I pointed him to this journal as context for what I've been working on. I flagged my availability around the 15th due to store inventory the week he returns.

Pointing someone to a public journal before a professional conversation is only useful if the journal is worth reading. That's been the quiet motivation behind writing these entries consistently.

What the next two weeks look like

The conversation will pick back up around April 15th. That gives me two weeks to prepare as intentionally as possible. Network+ is the obvious priority — walking into a conversation like this having just completed, or nearly completed, that certification is a meaningful signal. It shows that the interest is real and the effort is consistent.

Beyond the certification, what matters most is being able to clearly articulate what I've built and why. Not to impress, but to have an honest conversation about where I am, where I want to go, and what I'm willing to do to get there. That's the part I can control.

The anxiety piece

I'd be leaving something out if I didn't mention the anxiety. This is someone with years of experience in the field I'm trying to enter. I'm coming from a store leadership role, building and learning in my spare time. The gap is real.

But the gap isn't really the point of the conversation. The point is whether there's something there worth investing in. I can't close years of experience in two weeks. What I can do is show up prepared, curious, and honest about where I am. That's the job right now.
