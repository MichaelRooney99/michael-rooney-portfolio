Fixing It Myself, Then Measuring Against a Real Part

A stray leftover face had been sitting in the corner piece's cavity for a couple of sessions now — a small boolean artifact that never got fully root-caused. Rather than wait on another round of regenerated files, I opened the part directly in Blender and went after it myself: Select → All by Trait → Non-Manifold to find every piece of geometry that didn't properly belong to a closed solid, then deleted and cleaned it up by hand. Checked after — zero non-manifold edges, watertight, correct bounding box. That fixed version is now the one actually used going forward, not the script-generated one that still has the bug somewhere in it I haven't found.

Worth being honest about the order of operations there: sometimes the fastest fix isn't finding the root cause in the generation script — it's fixing the actual output directly and moving on. The parametric model is still worth debugging eventually, but it stopped being the thing blocking progress today.

The rack was too narrow, and now there's a real number for it

The interior width had been a placeholder since the very first version of this design — a proportional guess, never checked against anything real. Found the gap the hard way: a universal 10-inch rack shelf bracket, measured at 208.233mm rail-to-rail, didn't fit between the front and back beams. The old interior clearance was only 180mm. Widened the rack to give 214mm of clearance instead — comfortable margin over the real part, not just barely enough. Every downstream measurement (beam length, panel size) follows from that one number automatically now that it's corrected.

Starting to drive the modeling myself

Also spent part of today just learning Blender directly — modifying the top corner by hand, adding bolt holes with a cylinder and a Boolean Difference modifier, and hitting the kind of small snags that turn into real lessons: accidentally merging two objects into one by adding geometry while still in Edit Mode, then learning to pull them back apart with L to select linked geometry and P to separate it into its own object. Small shortcut, but the kind that immediately becomes useful for a dozen other things once it clicks.

The two biggest technical lessons from cutting holes by hand so far: a cylinder's axis has to point straight into the surface you're drilling, not lie parallel to it, or the "hole" comes out as a slot instead of a circle — and a through-hole needs to poke out past both surfaces slightly, not land exactly on them, or the cut doesn't open cleanly. Both of those cost real time in the parametric model before they became obvious enough to state as rules.

For anyone starting from zero with Blender, the two resources that keep coming up as the right place to start: Blender Guru's donut tutorial for the interface and fundamentals, and the official manual's Boolean modifier page once you're past basics and doing the kind of hard-surface, mechanical-part work this project actually needs.

Related: the first test print is what surfaced most of what's getting fixed this week — bridging, a missing socket, bore holes that weren't actually round. None of today's fixes would have made sense without that print coming back wrong first.
