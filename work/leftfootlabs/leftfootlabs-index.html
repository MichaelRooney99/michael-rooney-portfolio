leftfootLabs

Small, 3D-printed 5U 10-inch homelab rack kits for homelabbers without access to a printer of their own — a parametric Blender design turned into something other people in the homelab community can actually use, deployed at leftfootlabs.com.

Overview

What this is

leftfootLabs makes small 3D-printed rack kits for people running a homelab who don't own a printer of their own — a 5U, 10-inch open-frame rack, sized for the kind of gear most homelabbers actually have on hand: mini PCs, small switches, patch panels, a compact UPS. The markup is modest by design. This isn't meant to be a business so much as a way to hand off something that already works.

The design itself has been through real iteration, most of it driven by actual test prints rather than getting everything right on a screen first. Early passes used a cage-nut rail system, borrowed from commercial racks, before switching to fixed M6 heat-set inserts — simpler, fewer loose parts, and a better fit for a kit meant to be assembled by hand. The frame is modular: posts stack in segments to grow from 5U to 8U or 10U without a redesign, and corners are split from beams so each piece stays small enough to print reliably and swap out on its own. The base panel carries the actual leftfootLabs mark, rendered as a honeycomb vent — solid everywhere else, open cells cut into the shape of the logo itself.

None of that came from getting the CAD right on the first try. A handful of early prints came back with real problems — a joint that printed as two separate pieces instead of one, support material trapped somewhere unreachable, a fix that solved one problem and quietly broke another. Working through those is most of what the updates below actually document.

leftfootLabs is also the first real testbed for the Rack Planner, a separate tool — still in scoping, not yet built — for laying out a homelab build and checking that a kit like this actually fits before anyone spends money on one.
