Session 25 — Closing Phase 2: A Database, a Decision, and a Comparison, All Made Testable

18-Automated Test Coverage's Phase 2 had three items left on it — db.ts's day-bucketing, the auto-drafted-incident threshold logic in poller.ts, and nudge.ts's own signature comparison. All three closed this session, each for a genuinely different reason, which is worth writing down rather than treating as one undifferentiated "add tests" task.

Testing a real database instead of mocking one

getDayBucketedHistory isn't a pure function — it queries a real SQLite connection. Mocking that connection would only prove "the right SQL got called," not that the actual worst-of-day rollup logic behaves correctly. Instead, the test suite points node:sqlite at :memory: for the whole run — a real database, fully isolated, fast enough that the tradeoff barely exists. Nine tests came out of it: single readings mapping to the right day, no-data days staying honest rather than guessed, the worst-of-day rollup actually picking the worst reading regardless of what order they arrived in, and a specific edge case worth calling out — an unknown reading is deliberately excluded from the severity ranking entirely, not treated as some low-severity status. A day with only an unknown reading shows no-data, same as a day with nothing recorded at all, and an unknown reading sitting alongside a real outage the same day doesn't hide that outage.

Extracting a decision so it can be asked a question directly

The auto-incident threshold logic was buried inside a function that also queried the database twice and wrote to it — testing it as it was would have meant testing all of that machinery just to reach one small decision. Pulled the actual decision out into its own function, decideIncidentAction, taking exactly the two facts the decision depends on — recent readings, and whether an incident's already active — and returning one of three plain outcomes. No database, no mocking, just a function you can ask a direct question and check the answer. The original function still exists, now just orchestration: fetch the two facts, ask the pure function what to do, act on the answer.

Eight tests came out of that extraction, and the two that matter most aren't the obvious "outage drafts an incident" cases — they're the two that guard against the wrong kind of eagerness. A service flapping between outage and operational readings shouldn't draft a new incident on a single bad tick, and it shouldn't get auto-resolved early just because its most recent reading happened to look fine. Both directions, covered explicitly.

The same pattern, a third time, for a different reason

nudge.ts's signature comparison had a real but subtle correctness question buried in it: what should happen on the very first check after a proxy restart, when there's no previous signature to compare against at all? Treating a missing baseline as "the status changed" would broadcast a nudge to every connected client the moment the proxy comes back online — even though nothing has actually changed, there's just nothing yet to compare. Extracted into compareSignature, which treats that case as its own outcome, 'initial', distinct from both 'changed' and 'unchanged'. Three tests, small function, real correctness question it exists specifically to answer.

Where the numbers land

34 tests in the proxy now — 17 in nudge.test.ts, 9 in db.test.ts, 8 in poller.test.ts — alongside the 32 already covering the client. 66 total, and every one of them targets something that's either already caused a real bug in this project, or represents a genuine correctness question rather than coverage for its own sake. That's 18-'s Phase 2 fully closed. Phase 3 — component and integration tests — stays exactly where it was: real, useful, and deliberately not urgent.

One practical unlock worth naming: 19-Deployment Testing and Playbook Safety's Layer 4 — gating a deploy on the test suite actually passing — was explicitly waiting on this. Gating a deploy against 23 client-only tests was always a thinner safety net than gating it against tests that also cover the proxy that same deploy is shipping — including, now, the exact check that would have caught the real nut_exporter/nut drift this project already hit once. That path's open now.
